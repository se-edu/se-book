<span id="title">Changing requirements</span>

<span id="prereqs"></span>

<span id="outcomes">{{ icon_outcome }} Can explain why requirements change and what follows from that</span>

<div id="body">

**Requirements are not settled once and then left alone; they keep changing while the product is being built.** Three reasons account for most of it:

1. **Stakeholders work out what they want by seeing something concrete.**<br>
   {{ label_example }} %%A user who could not describe the report they needed can say exactly what is wrong with the first version of it.%%
1. **The world the product lives in changes.**<br>
   {{ label_example }} %%A new regulation comes into force, or a competitor ships a feature that users now expect.%%
1. **The team learns what things actually cost.**<br>
   {{ label_example }} %%A requirement turns out to take ten times the effort everyone assumed, which changes whether it is worth having at all.%%

Two things follow from this.

**A specification that is not kept up to date does not become harmless, it becomes misleading**, because people keep acting on it. A developer builds the version that was superseded a month ago; a tester reports a defect against behavior nobody wants any more.

**Every change is also a decision about what will not be built.** Accepting a new requirement late competes with requirements already agreed, so priorities have to be revisited rather than settled once at the start.
